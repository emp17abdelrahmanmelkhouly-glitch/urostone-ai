EAU Guidelines on Urolithiasis • Clinical Reference Compendium

European Association of Urology Guidelines Office • Decision Rules & Evidence Levels
1. Emergency Decompression in Sepsis & Solitary Kidney (EAU § 3.2.3) LoE 1b • Strong Recommendation

For patients with sepsis, fever (>38°C), systemic inflammatory response (SIRS), or infected obstructed hydronephrosis, urgent decompression of the collecting system is mandatory.

  • Decompression Modality: Urgent placement of a Retrograde Double-J Stent or Percutaneous Nephrostomy (PCN) combined with immediate broad-spectrum intravenous antimicrobials.
  • Contraindication: Definitive stone fragmentation (URS / SWL) is strictly contraindicated during active acute infection.
  • Solitary Kidney: An obstructed solitary kidney with rising creatinine requires urgent decompression to avert irreversible anuric acute renal injury.

3. Medical Expulsive Therapy (MET) Protocol (EAU § 3.4.3) LoE 1a • Strong Recommendation

Offer alpha-blockers as Medical Expulsive Therapy (MET) for patients with distal ureteral stones > 5 mm (or 4–7 mm) who are candidates for conservative therapy.

  • First-Line Posology: Tamsulosin 0.4 mg PO once daily (or Silodosin 8 mg daily).
  • Documented Benefits: Significantly higher stone expulsion rates, reduced time to expulsion, decreased colic episodes, and reduced analgesic consumption.
  • Patient Counseling: Counsel patients regarding potential side effects including retrograde ejaculation, orthostatic hypotension, and nasal congestion.
  • Stones < 4mm: Routine MET is not indicated for stones < 4 mm due to absence of demonstrable clinical superiority over placebo.
4. The 4-Week Surveillance Ceiling & Nephron Protection (EAU § 3.4.1) EAU Clinical Safety Mandate

A trial of conservative observation must NOT exceed 4 to 6 weeks.

  • Observation with periodic imaging is appropriate only while pain is controlled, with no signs of sepsis, and preserved renal function.
  • Renal Damage Risk: Persistent complete or high-grade ureteral obstruction exceeding 4 to 6 weeks causes progressive and irreversible reduction in glomerular filtration rate (nephron loss) and stricture formation.
  • Periodic Imaging: Serial ultrasound or low-dose non-contrast CT must be performed at 2 to 4 weeks if the calculus has not been retrieved.
5. Active Stone Removal Modality Selection: URS vs SWL (EAU § 3.4.4) LoE 1a • Strong Recommendation
  • Distal Ureter: Ureteroscopy (URS / semi-rigid laser lithotripsy) is the preferred treatment (superior stone-free rate in a single procedure compared to SWL, LoE 1a).
  • Mid & Proximal Ureter:
    • If Stone < 10 mm and CT Attenuation < 1000 HU: SWL and URS are both valid first-line options. SWL is non-invasive; URS offers faster clearance.
    • If Stone > 10 mm OR CT Attenuation ≥ 1000 HU (Brushite, COM): URS (flexible RIRS with Holmium/Thulium laser) is strongly preferred over SWL due to high stone density and shockwave resistance.
  • Analgesia: EAU § 3.1.2: First-line analgesia is NSAIDs (Diclofenac 50mg, Ibuprofen 400mg, Ketorolac, or Metamizole) with superior pain reduction and fewer adverse effects compared to opioids (LoE 1b, Strong).
